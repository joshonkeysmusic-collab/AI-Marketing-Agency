**HOOK**
The scariest AI answer isn't the wrong one. It's the convincing one.

**BODY**
Two things move in opposite directions every time someone leans on AI for a fast decision.

AI confidence goes up. Models are built to be helpful — faced with a gap, they don't say "I don't know," they produce the most plausible-sounding answer. Fluent, fast, confident.

Human scrutiny goes down. The more often the machine is right, the less anyone checks the next answer. Researchers call it automation bias — a halo effect around anything that sounds authoritative.

Where those two lines cross is where the real risk lives. Not in AI being wrong. In nobody being left to catch it.

**TAKEAWAY**
The real question isn't "is the AI right?"

It's "would I have caught it if it wasn't?"
