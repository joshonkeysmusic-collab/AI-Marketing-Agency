AI & decision-making · Macro

The scariest AI answer
isn't wrong.
It's convincing.

Two forces are moving in opposite directions inside every fast AI-assisted decision. Where they cross is where the real risk lives.

Rising

AI confidence

Models are optimised to be helpful. Faced with a gap, they don't say "I don't know" — they produce the most plausible-sounding answer. Fluent, fast, confident.

Falling

Human scrutiny

Under time pressure, people stop critically checking machine output and start passively approving it. Researchers call it automation bias — a halo effect around anything that sounds authoritative.

CROSS
-OVER

Why the lines cross

1

Compliance bias: a model built to be helpful will guess rather than admit uncertainty — a wrong answer stated as fact.

2

Automation bias: the more often the machine is right, the less anyone checks the next answer — including the one it gets wrong.

3

The result: speed and confidence compound, quietly, until the one answer nobody double-checked is the one that mattered.

The real question isn't "is the AI right?"
It's "would I have caught it if it wasn't?"
